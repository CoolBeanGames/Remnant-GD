# Fictional newsprint scan atlas

newsprint_scan_atlas.png is an original bitmap generated with the built-in ImageGen
tool on 2026-10-11. It supplies the print on real Blender paper geometry; it is not
a substitute for the room renders. The user's reference informed the dense print,
overlapping collage and torn openings. Its graffiti, people and scene were not
copied. This file is versioned and packed into the room native source and GLB.

The 2 × 2 atlas contains four fictional inside pages. studio_newspaper_layout.py
chooses reproducible partial-page UV crops, models wrinkled/curled edges, overlaps
48 clippings and cuts geometric holes. Source art remains unmodified.

## Exact generation prompt

Create a single high-resolution square texture atlas for an actual 3D game asset, showing four DIFFERENT vintage newspaper page scans, in a precise 2 by 2 layout, with no gutter or background: every quadrant filled edge to edge with its newspaper page. Orthographic completely flat scanner view, perfectly even diffuse light, no perspective, no physical folds, no torn edges, no shadows from objects, no vignette. The 4 pages are fictional late 1970s / early 1980s small-city broadsheet INSIDE pages, black-and-grey monochrome ink on pale dirty grey-white newsprint, only very faint yellowing. Realistic small dense serif text in 6 to 8 narrow columns, many smaller multi-column articles, tiny section headings, assorted classifieds and a few grainy monochrome halftone editorial photos of anonymous streets/buildings, a portrait and mundane adverts. Every page has a different nonuniform editorial layout. No huge repeated title banners, no huge blank spaces, no word 'NEWSPAPER', no red writing, no graffiti, no large readable slogan, no recognisable brands, no watermark. Fine realistic newsprint paper fibres, faded black ink and subtle show-through from the reverse. This is the source print atlas to UV-map onto independently modelled wrinkled paper clippings over apartment windows, inspired by neglected horror-game newspaper window coverings. Aim for photographic newspaper scan texture, not stylised typesetting or a geometric illustration. Keep the fine print subordinate; it should read as dense authentic print texture at a distance.
